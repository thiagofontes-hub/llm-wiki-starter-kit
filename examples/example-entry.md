Nemawashi (根回し, Japanese: "going around the roots") is the practice of having individual, informal conversations with key decision-makers *before* a major meeting or presentation — so that by the time you're in the room, buy-in is largely already secured.

The failure mode it prevents: preparing a polished strategic deck and presenting it to a group of 40 people for the first time, only to have it torn apart by questions and objections you could have anticipated. Pitching cold to a large group is one of the highest-risk communication situations. You have no idea what concerns exist, who is opposed, or who has adjacent context that changes your proposal.

Nemawashi inverts this. Before the big meeting:
- Meet individually with the major decision-makers and allies from your [[influence-map]]
- Give them a preview: share what you're proposing, get their reaction, address their concerns
- Explicitly ask their input: "Does this make sense in your world? Is there anything you'd want me to address?"
- Incorporate their feedback where appropriate

By the time the large meeting happens, your key stakeholders have already processed the idea. Their remaining questions are likely minor refinements, not fundamental objections. And because they've been consulted, they tend to become advocates rather than critics.

The benefit extends beyond the meeting: asking for input before presenting signals respect for others' knowledge, builds [[trust-capital]], and converts potential opponents into collaborators.

The rule of thumb: **do your pushing ahead of time in small rooms, not for the first time in the big room.**
